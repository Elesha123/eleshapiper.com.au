You know your subject better than almost anyone in the room. So why does the cursor blink for twenty minutes every time you open LinkedIn to post?

Here's what's actually going on: the things you find obvious are the things your clients most need to hear. You've stopped being able to tell the difference between "everyone knows this" and "this is the insight that gets you hired."

## The real blocker isn't writing skill

Most consultants who go quiet aren't bad writers. They're too close to their own expertise to know what's worth saying. Every idea feels either too basic to share or too complex to explain in a scroll-stopping post — and that tension is exactly what keeps good people invisible.

A narrow content focus beats a broad one every time. Pick the three or four problems you solve on repeat, and only write about those.

- Write down the last three questions a client asked you in a discovery call
- Turn each one into a post that answers it in public
- Notice which one gets the most replies — that's your next pillar

> The posts that perform aren't the clever ones. They're the ones that name a problem your reader didn't know how to describe.

### Start with the client's words, not yours

The fastest way past the blank page is to stop trying to sound impressive and start transcribing what you already say out loud — on calls, in proposals, in the margins of a client's brief.
